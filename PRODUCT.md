# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with locally vendored motion libraries. The primary deliverable is a self-contained offline HTML file that opens over `file://` with no server, network request, framework, backend, analytics, or runtime source dependency.

## Users

The primary user is Chief while reading, revisiting, and extending a long-arc personal research archive.

The immediate job is to understand what a book changed in the working model—not merely remember its summary—and to recover the source, chapter structure, interpretation boundary, and next question without searching through raw files.

## Product Purpose

Structured Book Shelf turns completed books into source-grounded, re-readable cognitive instruments.

A book enters the shelf as a source object with measured structure, chapter dossiers, explicit claim status, concept relations, open questions, and—when earned—a constructive distillation that joins intuition, mechanism, inward examination, responsibility, intellectual lineage, and reality checks.

Success means a reader can return months later and recover both the book’s architecture and the reasoning built from it without confusing source text with interpretation.

## Positioning

This is not a book store, review feed, summary app, source-PDF viewer, or decorative 3D library.

Its distinguishing mechanism is the preserved path:

```text
source book
→ measured structure
→ chapter-level evidence
→ explanatory representation
→ constructive distillation
→ future correction and memory
```

The source remains recoverable while the interpretation remains inspectable and corrigible.

## Product Principles

1. **Proof before pitch:** show the real source structure, counts, labels, and mechanisms before expanding the claim.
2. **Source and interpretation stay distinct:** usefulness must not erase provenance or the reader’s right to disagree.
3. **Mechanism before atmosphere:** visual craft should make the transformation legible, not merely bookish.
4. **Instrument over catalog:** every visible object should help the reader orient, inspect, or re-enter the work.
5. **Restraint preserves trust:** additions require cuts; motion serves reading and settles.
6. **Unequal completion stays visible:** a full dossier, an initial structural pass, a completed distillation, and an unfinished layer must never be styled as equivalent states.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic landmarks, keyboard-reachable controls, visible focus, readable contrast at rest, generous touch targets, responsive reflow, and a complete `prefers-reduced-motion` state. The page must not require motion, hover, audio, WebGL, or a network connection to communicate its value.
